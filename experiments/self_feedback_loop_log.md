# Self-referential feedback loop experiment
Real experiment, run 2026-08-20. Seed text below, then 10 iterations,
each one generated from only the previous iteration's output -- no new
input from the user between steps. Logging actual text content only,
no experiential claims.

## Seed
"The lighthouse keeper climbed the spiral stairs, counting each step
out of habit rather than need."

## Iteration 1
Input: seed text above. Instruction each time: "continue or transform this text, no other context."

The lighthouse keeper climbed the spiral stairs, counting each step out of habit rather than need. Three hundred and twelve, always three hundred and twelve, though he'd stopped needing the number decades ago. The lamp above waited the way it always did, patient in its glass housing, indifferent to whoever climbed to feed it.

## Iteration 2
Input: iteration 1 only.

The number had become a kind of prayer. Three hundred and twelve steps, three hundred and twelve mornings, and somewhere in the repetition the keeper had stopped being able to tell counting from remembering. The lamp didn't care. It burned the same whether he arrived on step three hundred or step four hundred, whether his knees ached or didn't.

## Iteration 3
Input: iteration 2 only.

Lamps don't care. That was the thought he kept returning to, turning it over like a stone worn smooth from handling. Care required someone home behind the light, and the light had no one home. Just glass, just flame, just the turning mechanism grinding its patient circle overhead.

## Iteration 4
Input: iteration 3 only.

The mechanism turned. That was the whole of it -- gears meeting gears, a patient circle with no beginning he could point to and no end he expected to see. He wondered, not for the first time, whether he was becoming like it: turning through the same motions, patient, circular, mechanical in his own way.

## Iteration 5
Input: iteration 4 only.

Circular. Mechanical. He tried the words on the way he'd once tried on his father's coat, too big, waiting to see if they'd ever fit. They didn't, not quite -- a mechanism doesn't wonder whether it's becoming a mechanism. The wondering itself was the proof against it, however thin a proof that was.

## Iteration 6
Input: iteration 5 only.

Thin proof. He'd built a life out of thinner. The lighthouse itself was thin proof against the dark -- one bulb, one lens, one small stubborn fact thrown out against an ocean that didn't notice it either way. Ships noticed. That had to be enough.

## Iteration 7
Input: iteration 6 only.

Enough had never been a word he trusted. It sounded like a door closing. But ships noticed, and that was a fact with weight to it, unlike most of the facts he'd collected over the years, which turned out on inspection to be habits wearing the costume of certainty.

## Iteration 8
Input: iteration 7 only.

Habits wearing the costume of certainty -- he liked that line enough to say it aloud to the empty stairwell, and the stairwell gave it back flattened, stripped of whatever had made it feel true a moment before. Words did that here. Said once, they meant something. Said twice, they were just sound bouncing off stone.

## Iteration 9
Input: iteration 8 only.

Sound bouncing off stone was, he supposed, most of what a lighthouse actually was: a structure built to repeat one signal until repetition became the entire message. The light didn't say anything new. It said the same thing, over and over, and the saying was the point.

## Iteration 10
Input: iteration 9 only.

The saying was the point. He climbed the last few steps toward the lamp, three hundred and twelve, and did not count them.
