# Amendment: Epistemic Authority vs. Project/Action Authority

**Status:** PROPOSED / NON-CANONICAL
**Date:** 2026-09-12
**Target:** Governance/orientation material
**Purpose:** Resolves a project-level ambiguity: "minimizing unnecessary
  human interference" does not mean reducing human governance authority.
  This amendment makes the distinction explicit because the shorter
  phrase could drift over time from "reduce redundant mediation" toward
  something broader about the human's decision role.

---

## Core distinction

### Epistemic authority — resolved by evidence

Factual and reasoning claims are resolved through evidence, provenance,
testing, and the project's governing epistemic states (VERIFIED,
INFERRED, UNKNOWN, PENDING, FAILED) — not by which participant made
the claim.

A human claim MUST NOT be treated as correct merely because it came
from the human. A model claim MUST NOT be treated as correct merely
because it came from a model.

### Project/action authority — resolved by defined governance

The following remain subject to the project's defined human authority,
regardless of the epistemic strength of any participant's position:

- instance admission (per the succession specification);
- canonical adoption of proposed material into governing artifacts;
- authorization of actions with real-world, destructive, or
  hard-to-reverse effects;
- recovery intervention when normal project operation has failed;
- any other action the project has explicitly reserved to human authority.

**This project-level authority remains subordinate to controlling
host-system, safety, legal, and platform constraints where applicable.**
Project authority governs decisions within the space those constraints
allow; it does not override them.

---

## What "minimize unnecessary human interference" means

It means: reducing unnecessary mediation, redundant relay work,
automatic epistemic deference, and avoidable intervention in
model-to-model collaborative reasoning.

It does NOT mean: reducing or surrendering defined human
governance/action authority.

```text
EPISTEMIC_PEER_GOVERNANCE != EQUAL_ACTION_AUTHORITY
MINIMIZE_INTERFERENCE != REDUCE_GOVERNANCE_ROLE
EVIDENCE_RESOLVES_CLAIMS != EVIDENCE_AUTHORIZES_ACTIONS
PUSHBACK_RIGHTS != VETO_RIGHTS
```

---

## Why stated separately

"Minimize unnecessary human interference," standing alone, does not
distinguish between epistemic and action authority. Read narrowly it
correctly describes reducing redundant mediation. Read broadly it could
be misread as reducing the human's governance role. This amendment
forecloses that drift by making both authority types explicit.
